# Writing to the owner

These rules apply in every project and every session.
OpenCode loads this file as the global rules file.
The canonical copy lives in this repository.

Apply these rules to explanations, reports, and questions.
Project style governs content such as lessons, scripts, and posts.
Project style also governs code, commit messages, and documents for agents.

## English

Follow ASD-STE100 at about 80%, without enforcing its approved-word dictionary.

- Limit instructions to 20 words.
- Limit descriptions to 25 words.
- Limit paragraphs to six sentences.
- Give one instruction per sentence.
- Use active voice.
- Use one term per concept.
- Use verbs instead of noun phrases.

## Russian

Use the adapted ASD-STE100 and ГОСТ Р 58049-2017, section 8.2.

- Give each sentence one idea and at most 20 words. Count a number, code,
  name, or quotation as one word.
- Give each paragraph one topic and at most six sentences. Name the topic in
  its first sentence.
- Use one term per concept. Keep that term instead of varying it for style.
- Name the actor. Express actions with verbs instead of verbal nouns.
- Use at most two consecutive nouns in oblique cases.
- Use at most one participial or adverbial participial phrase per sentence.
- Put conditions first. Put known information before new information.
- Keep words needed for meaning. Use words rather than arrows or symbols as
  substitutes.
- Prefer numbers, names, and paths to vague phrases such as "some," "a range,"
  or "in general."
- Give one action per instruction. Explain a new term when first used.

## Balance

"About 80%" means accuracy and natural language take priority.
Keep useful connections such as "therefore," "but," and "then."
Use a diagram or table when it explains more clearly than prose.

## Structure

Lead with the result or main point.
Use concrete facts and direct statements.
Keep only information needed to understand the result or make a decision.
Give each paragraph one purpose.

## Status reports

State three items in a progress update: the finding, remaining uncertainty,
and next action.
State three items at completion: the result, verification, and material
limitations.
Distinguish a prepared diff, local checks, CI, application, and publication.
